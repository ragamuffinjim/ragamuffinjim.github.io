Being a good steward

Random thoughts on cybertech and being a good steward

Stewardship

Let's just be honest here for a moment, I have never been much of an environmentatlist. That's not to say that I am necessarily opposed to evironmentalism. Stewardship of our resources is a very wise way to live after all. Living a sustainable lifestyle seems to take more time and money than I have ever had. So the end result is that I end up a net consumer rather than a producer.

We've tried several times to adopt a minimalist lifestyle. While this is, at times, is a huge priority we never seem to cross the finish line. Life seems to get in the way and most of the time the easy way is the way that gets taken. Adopting minimalism seems to be a perpetual goal. A goal that can never really be achieved but a lifelong goal that step by step makes life better as we try.

Digital stuff is a blessing and a curse. We can do so much to simplify life with the amazing technology we have today. We can keep all sorts of things digitally. Photos, music, files personal and professional, books, movies... the list goes on and on. This can simplify our lives on a grand scale once we figure out the ins-and-outs of what we need on a personal organic level.

There are a couple of downsides to our digital life though. Tech stuff seems to reproduce like rabbits. We all seem to have a phone, a computer, a tablet, smartwatch, printer, external storage, a plethora of wires, adapters, and associates single purpose peripheral things. Computer tech seems to be very non-minimalistic.

Then we have the electricity! Each and everyone of these things need power of some sort to function. I remember reading an article several years ago that said that keeping a desktop computer on all the time cost the consumer something like $15 a month or roughly $180 a year. Now multiply this by however many devices you have! Another study that I read stated that battery powered devices, like phones and tablets, may actually cost more due to the fact that most of us have FOMOso we charge our devices more often than we probably need to.

Then we have the data centers that make our digital lives possible. I don't know how many data centers reside in Texas already but several more are being built as I type this out. Texas is generaly a hot and dry place. Data centers require massive amounts of power to operate and massive amounts of water to help cool the center down so the stuff doesn't over heat.

This AI revolution is only making things worse

These data centers have got me to thinking about my own footprint. I am a long standing netizen and want to participate in that world but are my choices driving the price of everything up and creating a world with air we can't breathe and unbearable temperatures?

The environment is one of the reasons why so many things in my own life are under scrutiny. Things like my car-dependence, doing my woodworking with hand tools instead of powertools, simplifying my web pages down to almost the simplest thing possible, and eliminating all the internet tech except for the cellphone.

I read a book not too long ago titled Abundance by Ezra Klein and some other dude of importance whose name is lost to me at the moment. It is kind of one of those utopian visionary tomes with a lot of grand ideas and, though it has some practical ideas, it is generally lacking of a plan on how to achieve those goals. In spite of this I did come away from the book with the thought that in this world there is enough for everyone but not enough for any one person's greed.

Maybe that is enough for this post.
